How the indicator works

The indicator has 5 detection engines running simultaneously on every bar. Each engine scans the last N pivot points for its pattern. When all rules pass → label appears. When any rule fails → nothing appears.

5
Pattern detectors
3
Alert conditions
9

What appears on chart

5 things drawn on chart

  • 1Green/red diamonds at every pivot high/low
  • 2Dashed pattern boxes (FLAT, TERMINAL, etc.) with wave labels (a:3, b:3, c:5)
  • 3Red dashed key invalidation level line
  • 4Fibonacci retrace/extension lines from last swing
  • 5Info panel showing RSI, bias, active patterns

What it does NOT do

  • ✗Does NOT repaint — labels appear only when all rules confirm
  • ✗Does NOT give buy/sell arrows — you read the pattern context
  • ✗Does NOT predict — it identifies current pattern state
  • ✗Does NOT work on 1m/5m — designed for Weekly/Daily/4H

Row-by-row meaning

  • RRSI(14) — Shows current RSI value. "OB" appended means ≥70 (overbought). "OS" means ≤30 (oversold). Confirm patterns: Flat c:5 entry is best when RSI is NOT overbought.
  • KKey Level — Your configured invalidation price. Color matches whether price is above (teal) or below (red) this level.
  • BBias — Simple: ✓ BULLISH = price above key level. ✗ BEARISH = price below. Do NOT take longs when bias shows bearish.
  • PPivots — How many pivots the indicator has detected. Below 8 pivots → Diametric cannot detect. Below 6 → Terminal cannot detect. Need at least 20+ pivots for reliable scanning.

💡 Key insight about "Scanning" The panel always shows "Scanning" when enabled — it does not say "Detected" or "Active." Look at the CHART for actual pattern labels (the boxes and wave letter labels). The panel is just your dashboard for bias and RSI confirmation.
Pattern Signal

Flat Correction signal

Detected when the last 4 pivots form a:3 → b:3 → c:5. The green dashed box appears with "FLAT" label and three wave labels. This is what Bitcoin is currently doing.

Flat detection — what appears on chart
What VALIDATES the signal
  • ✓Wave A is a clear 3-wave structure (up-down-up for bullish flat)
  • ✓Wave B retraces between 61.8% and 138.2% of Wave A
  • ✓Wave C begins a 5-wave impulse structure beyond A's end
  • ✓Price is above the key level ($66,800 for BTC)
  • ✓RSI is NOT overbought (below 70) when entering c:5
  • ✓C-wave impulse fits in an expanding channel
What INVALIDATES the signal
  • ✗Wave B retraces MORE than 138.2% of Wave A → not a flat
  • ✗Wave B retraces LESS than 61.8% of Wave A → it's a Zigzag instead
  • ✗Wave C fails to extend beyond Wave A's end
  • ✗Price closes below the key level (b:3 goes too deep)
  • ✗Wave C shows only 3 sub-waves (would make it a different pattern)
  • ✗RSI hits 70+ at c:5 start with divergence (terminal risk)

How to trade around the Flat signal

LONG
Enter long at the end of b:3 wave
When b:3 label appears and price is above key level → this is the c:5 launch point. Fibonacci: look for b:3 to end near 61.8–100% retrace of a:3.
STOP
Stop loss: just below the key invalidation level
For BTC Flat: stop below $66,800. If b:3 closes below this → the flat is invalid, exit immediately.
TARGET
Target: 127.2% or 161.8% extension of Wave a beyond b:3 end
Use the Fibonacci extension lines the indicator draws. The 161.8% line is the primary c:5 target (Bitcoin ~$160k area).
Pattern Signal

Diametric signal

Detected when last 8 pivots form 7 swings where odd waves progressively grow (expanding) or shrink (contracting). Labels: a b c d e f g. Title: "EXPANDING DIAMETRIC ◇" or "CONTRACTING DIAMETRIC ◇".

EXPANDING — validates when
  • ✓Wave c is larger than wave a (same direction)
  • ✓Wave e is larger than wave c
  • ✓Wave g is larger than wave e
  • ✓Even waves (b, d, f) are smaller than adjacent odd waves
  • ✓All 7 segments alternate direction cleanly
INVALIDATES when
  • ✗Any odd wave is smaller than the previous odd wave
  • ✗Any even wave is larger than its adjacent odd waves
  • ✗Pattern has fewer or more than 7 segments
  • ✗Waves do not alternate direction cleanly
  • ✗Chart doesn't have enough pivots (need Pivots ≥ 8)

Trading the Diametric

WAIT
Do not trade inside the Diametric — wait for g-wave completion
The pattern is complex and corrective. Enter AFTER g-wave completes. The move following a Diametric is typically a strong impulse.
ENTRY
Enter in the direction of the prior trend after g-wave label appears
If prior trend was up (as in Bitcoin) → go long after g completes. If prior trend was down → go short. Use X-wave label as additional confirmation.
💡 Low pivot count warning If the Pivots row shows fewer than 12, the Diametric scanner may produce false signals. Increase Pivot Strength in settings until you have 15+ meaningful pivots on the chart.
Pattern Signal

Expanding Triangle signal

Detected when last 7 pivots show 6 swings where same-direction waves grow progressively (c>a, e>c). Draws diverging trendlines. Labels: i a b c d e. After completion → expect a major trend impulse.

Validates when
  • ✓Wave c price range is larger than wave a
  • ✓Wave e price range is larger than wave c
  • ✓The two trendlines (upper, lower) are diverging — moving apart
  • ✓At least 5–6 clear pivots form the pattern
Invalidates when
  • ✗Wave c is smaller than wave a (converging = regular triangle)
  • ✗Trendlines are parallel (symmetrical triangle, not expanding)
  • ✗Wave e fails to extend beyond c's extreme
  • ✗Pattern has only 4–5 pivots (need 6 for full structure)

Trading the Expanding Triangle

KEY MOMENT
e-wave completion is the signal
When the "e" label appears at the end of the fifth swing → the pattern is complete. The next move is typically the largest and fastest impulse in the prior trend direction.
STOP
Stop: below the e-wave low (for bullish triangle)
If price returns below e-wave's end → triangle is invalid. The breakout failed.
Reversal Signal — MOST IMPORTANT

Terminal Impulse signal

When the indicator detects a Terminal, it places a red box labeled "⚠ TERMINAL IMPULSE — Expect Reversal" with dashed converging channel lines. Sub-waves are labeled 1:3 2:3 3:3 4:3 5:3. This is the STRONGEST reversal signal in the indicator.

🔴 This pattern ends trends A confirmed Terminal means the current trend is exhausted. The reversal that follows is typically sharp and fast. On Bitcoin's chart, "TERMINAL?" near the top of c:5 means the entire bull run from b:3 low may be ending at that level.
Terminal — validation vs invalidation
Terminal VALIDATES when ALL of these:
  • ✓Wave 3 price range is SMALLER than Wave 1
  • ✓Wave 5 price range is SMALLER than Wave 3
  • ✓Wave 4 overlaps into Wave 1's price territory
  • ✓Wave 4 range is larger than Wave 2 range
  • ✓All 5 waves fit inside a converging wedge channel
  • ✓RSI shows divergence (making lower highs while price makes higher highs)
INVALIDATES — it's a normal impulse when:
  • ✗Wave 3 is LARGER than Wave 1 (normal impulse rule)
  • ✗Wave 4 does NOT overlap Wave 1 (normal impulse territory)
  • ✗The channel lines are parallel or expanding (not converging)
  • ✗Any sub-wave has a 5-wave structure (Terminal = all 3-wave sub-waves)
  • ✗Price breaks out above the upper channel line strongly

How to trade the Terminal signal

SHORT
Enter short after 5:3 wave completes and price breaks the lower channel line
Wait for the converging wedge to break down. Do not short prematurely — the 5:3 wave can extend. Confirmation = close below the lower channel line.
STOP
Stop: above the 5:3 wave high
If price makes a new high above the 5:3 label → Terminal is invalid, trend continues. Exit immediately.
TARGET
Minimum target: retrace to origin of the Terminal (where 1:3 started)
After a Terminal, price typically retraces 100% of the entire Terminal pattern quickly. Extended targets: 127.2% and 161.8% of Terminal length.
Pattern Label

X-Wave label

When the indicator finds a 50–79% shallow retracement of the prior swing, it places a small "x" label. This is a LABEL, not a trade signal by itself — it tells you that two corrective patterns are being connected.

X-Wave validates (labeled) when
  • ✓Prior corrective pattern (Flat, Zigzag, Triangle) has completed
  • ✓Retracement is 50% to 79% of the prior pattern's total size
  • ✓The retracement is a 3-wave structure internally
X-Wave invalidates when
  • ✗Retracement exceeds 100% of the prior pattern → it's a new pattern
  • ✗Retracement is less than 50% → probably part of the next pattern
  • ✗The "x" label appears without a preceding complete pattern (false pivot)

What to do when you see "x"

  • →Do NOT trade the X-wave itself — it is too small and ambiguous
  • →Expect ANOTHER corrective pattern after the X-wave ends (equal or larger than the first)
  • →The overall correction is not done yet — this is "complex correction" territory
  • →On BTC chart: the "g·x" label means after the Diametric's g-wave, a shallow bounce (x) connected to the Flat correction that followed
Key Level

Using the Key Invalidation Level

The most important single line in the indicator. Default: $66,800 for Bitcoin. Change it to your asset's critical structural level. Everything revolves around this price.

ABOVE
Panel shows BULLISH · Take longs
NEAR
±1.5% → Yellow candles · Caution zone
BELOW
Panel shows BEARISH · No longs

What the key level actually invalidates

For BTC Flat Correction ($66,800)
  • !If b:3 wave closes BELOW $66,800 → the Expanded Flat is invalid. The b:3 wave has retraced too much (more than the a:3 origin). Cannot be a Flat anymore.
  • !The pattern becomes either a Double Zigzag or a new bear market count → flip to short bias
  • !All active LONG positions should be closed or stopped immediately on this break

Setting the key level for your asset

1

Find the origin of the corrective pattern

The key level should be the price where Wave A started (for Flat) or the last major swing low (for Impulse). This is the point that if crossed means the pattern is structurally impossible.

2

Open Settings and enter the price

Settings → Key Level ($) → Enter your price. Settings → Key Level Label → Enter a short label like "Flat Invalidation" or "Bull Invalid".

3

Watch for the candle color change

When price closes below: entire bar turns red tint. When within ±1.5%: bar turns yellow. These are your visual alerts on the chart itself.

🔴 Never place a long stop BELOW the key level If price is heading to the key level, set your stop JUST below it (1–2%). If it breaks, the entire bullish wave count is wrong — not just the trade, the entire thesis.
Fibonacci Lines

Reading the Fibonacci lines

The indicator draws 9 Fibonacci levels automatically from the last completed swing high to swing low (or vice versa). They update on every new bar. Here is exactly what to do with each level.

// 9 levels drawn, in order from shallow to deep:
23.6% — Very shallow. Rarely significant. Strong trend continuation.
38.2% — Wave 4 of impulse typically ends here. Flat A-wave support.
50.0% — Psychological level. No NEoWave rule but often traded.
61.8% ★ KEY — Wave 2 of impulse, X-wave max, Flat B min. Most important.
78.6% — Deep correction. Sometimes Wave 2 in strong trends.
100.0% — Flat B-wave maximum for Regular Flat. Equal-leg target.
127.2% — Minimum C-wave extension beyond A. First target after Flat.
161.8% ★ KEY — Wave 3 target, C-wave primary target. Golden ratio extension.
261.8% — Extended Wave 3. Only in very strong trending markets.

Which level to use per scenario

  • FEntering Flat c:5 wave: Target 127.2% first, then 161.8% extension of the a:3 wave beyond b:3 end.
  • FWave 2 stop: If Wave 2 breaks below 61.8% retrace, wave count may be wrong. If below 100% → definitely wrong.
  • FWave 3 target: 161.8% of Wave 1 is the primary target. Plot this before entering.
  • FTerminal reversal target: After Terminal completes, first target is 61.8% retrace of the entire Terminal, then 100% retrace.
The Fibonacci lines update dynamically They are drawn from the last 2 pivots (swing high to swing low). As new pivots form, the lines shift. Use them as confluences — when a pattern rule AND a Fibonacci level align at the same price → high probability turning point.
Turn off extensions if chart is cluttered Settings → Fibonacci Extensions → OFF. This hides 127.2%, 161.8%, 261.8% lines. Keep retracements on to identify support/resistance.
Alerts

Setting up and using alerts

The indicator has 3 built-in alert conditions. To enable: click the alarm bell on any indicator label in TradingView, or go to Create Alert and select this indicator.

The 3 alert conditions

🔴
NEoWave: Price Broke Below Key Level
Fires ONCE when price closes below $66,800 (or your key level). This is your highest priority alert. Means the bullish Flat count is OVER. Action: close longs, reassess the full wave count from scratch.
⚠️
NEoWave: RSI Overbought — Terminal Warning
Fires when RSI ≥ 70. Does not mean sell immediately — but signals you're in the zone where a Terminal Impulse could be forming. Start watching for 5 shrinking sub-waves in the c:5 wave. Tighten stops.
🟢
NEoWave: RSI Oversold — Wave C Entry Zone
Fires when RSI ≤ 30. Coinciding with a b:3 wave label on chart → this is the ideal entry zone for the c:5 wave launch. Best alert for entries.

Recommended alert setup for BTC Flat trade

1

Alert 1 (mandatory): Key Level break

Condition: "NEoWave: Price Broke Below Key Level" → Once Per Bar Close → Notify on app + email. This protects your capital.

2

Alert 2 (entry): RSI Oversold

Condition: "NEoWave: RSI Oversold" → Once Per Bar → Notify on app. When this fires AND b:3 label is visible on chart → consider entering long.

3

Alert 3 (caution): RSI Overbought

Condition: "NEoWave: RSI Overbought" → Once Per Bar → Push notification. When this fires AND you see 5 small sub-waves forming in c:5 → possible Terminal forming, tighten stop.
