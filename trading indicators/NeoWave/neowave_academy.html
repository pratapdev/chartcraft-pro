Building Block

Impulse Wave — The Power Move

The core directional pattern. 5 waves in the trend direction, alternating with 3-wave corrections. The engine of all major bull and bear markets.

🧒 Think of a Staircase with Resting Spots

Imagine climbing a tall building using stairs. You walk up 3 flights (Wave 1 📈), rest briefly going down 1 flight (Wave 2 📉), run up 5 flights — the longest sprint! (Wave 3 📈), rest again going down 1 flight (Wave 4 📉), then finish climbing 2 more flights (Wave 5 📈). The market does exactly this — it cannot skip steps and has firm rules for each stair.

NEoWave Impulse Rules (ALL must be true)

  • Wave 2 must retrace at least 1% of Wave 1 but NEVER 100% or more
  • Wave 3 must be the longest OR one of the two longest waves. It can never be the shortest of waves 1, 3, and 5
  • Wave 4 must NOT overlap Wave 1's price territory (crucial NEoWave rule)
  • Alternation rule: Wave 2 and Wave 4 must look different (one sharp, one flat/complex)
  • The 5 waves must fit within a channel formed by connecting Wave 2 and 4 lows
  • Sub-wave structure: Waves 1, 3, 5 are 5-wave impulses; Waves 2, 4 are 3-wave corrections

Zigzag — The Sharp Correction (5-3-5)

The most common corrective pattern. A sharp, deep retracement that looks like a lightning bolt. Structure: A(5 waves) – B(3 waves) – C(5 waves)

🧒 Think of a Bouncing Ball Falling Down Stairs

You throw a ball down a staircase. It falls fast (Wave A 📉), bounces back up a little (Wave B 📈), then falls even further (Wave C 📉). The bounce-back in the middle is NEVER very big — always less than 61.8% of the first fall. This makes a sharp, pointed zig-zag shape — like a lightning bolt on the chart! Zigzags are the market's quick, violent correction moves.

Zigzag Correction — a(5) · b(3) · c(5) — Sharp, Deep Retracement

Zigzag Rules (NEoWave Strict)

  • Wave A must be 5 sub-waves (impulse structure)
  • Wave B retraces 38.2% to 61.8% of Wave A — never more than 61.8%
  • Wave C must be 5 sub-waves (impulse) — always extends beyond A's end
  • The entire pattern should be steep and sharp — like a lightning bolt
  • Time: Wave B is typically shorter in duration than Wave A
  • If B retraces more than 61.8% → it's a FLAT, not a Zigzag

🔑 How to Spot a Zigzag

Look for a sharp move down (or up), a small bounce, then another sharp move in the original direction. The whole thing should look like a steep "Z" or "N" shape. If the middle bounce is tiny (less than 61.8%), it's very likely a Zigzag.

Flat Correction — Sideways Battle (3-3-5)

The exact pattern Trading Heights is using on Bitcoin right now. A sideways correction where B-wave retraces almost all of A, followed by a powerful C-wave impulse. Structure: a(3) – b(3) – c(5)

🧒 Think of a Tug-of-War That Bulls Win

Imagine two teams pulling a rope. Bulls pull it up (Wave a 📈), Bears pull it almost all the way back (Wave b 📉 — almost undoing the bulls' work), then Bulls EXPLODE forward with a huge 5-wave charge (Wave c 📈📈📈📈📈). The key difference from a Zigzag: the middle pullback (B-wave) is MUCH BIGGER — almost going all the way back to the start. But then C launches powerfully beyond A's top!

Three Types of Flat Corrections

Regular Flat

B retraces ~100% of A. C ends at roughly A's end. Market moves sideways overall. Most common in consolidating markets.

Expanded Flat ⭐ Bitcoin

B exceeds A's starting point. C extends well beyond A's end. VERY common in crypto bull markets. Bitcoin is in this pattern NOW.

Running Flat

B far exceeds A. C barely retraces at all. Extremely bullish signal — the correction barely makes a dent. Very strong underlying trend.

⭐ NEoWave Exclusive Pattern

Diametric Formation — The 7-Wave Diamond

Completely unique to NEoWave. Not found in classical Elliott Wave at all. A 7-segment pattern (a-b-c-d-e-f-g) where alternating waves grow or shrink in a diamond shape. This is what preceded Bitcoin's current pattern.

🧒 Think of a Heartbeat Monitor Getting Louder, Then Quieter

You know how a heartbeat (ECG) looks on a hospital monitor? It goes beep… BEEP… BEEP… beep… beep (getting bigger then smaller). A Diametric is exactly like that — 7 waves that either get BIGGER each time (Expanding Diametric) or get SMALLER each time (Contracting Diametric). The pattern has 7 named waves: a, b, c, d, e, f, g — like the musical notes! It can only happen as a corrective pattern inside a larger trend.

Expanding Diametric — a·b·c·d·e·f·g — Each wave larger than the previous (same direction)
Up waves (a, c, e, g) — each larger
Down waves (b, d, f) — each larger
Expanding channel boundaries

Diametric Rules (NEoWave)

  • Exactly 7 segments labeled a through g. Not 5, not 9 — exactly 7.
  • Expanding: Each odd wave (a, c, e, g) is LARGER than the previous odd wave. Each even wave (b, d, f) is also progressively larger.
  • Contracting: Each odd wave is SMALLER than the previous. Pattern converges to a point.
  • Even waves must be smaller than the adjacent odd waves (b < a, d < c, f < e)
  • Pattern typically appears in complex corrections or as part of X-wave structures
  • After completion, a powerful impulse wave in the direction of the prior trend typically follows

Expanding Triangle — The Opening Fan

A 5-wave corrective pattern where each successive swing is LARGER than the previous. The channel lines diverge outward like an opening fan or megaphone shape. Labeled: i · a · b · c · d · e

🧒 Think of a Megaphone or Opening Fan

Have you ever seen someone use a megaphone? The shape starts narrow and gets wider. An Expanding Triangle is exactly that shape on a chart — each wave swing gets BIGGER and BIGGER. The two lines you draw through the highs and lows are moving APART (diverging) unlike a regular triangle where they converge. After this pattern completes, the market usually makes a VERY big move in the direction of the prior trend!

Expanding Triangle — i·a·b·c·d·e — Diverging channel (Megaphone shape)

Expanding Triangle Rules (NEoWave)

  • Each wave in the same direction must be LARGER than the previous (c > a, e > c)
  • Each wave in the opposite direction must also grow (d > b, which must be > i)
  • The two trendlines connecting the highs and lows must diverge — never parallel
  • Typically contains 5 sub-waves (i or a through e)
  • After completion → expect a large, violent move in the direction of the prior trend
  • This is what Bitcoin's structure before the Diametric was: an Expanding Triangle labeled i, a, b, c, d, e

📍 In the Bitcoin Chart

⭐ REVERSAL SIGNAL — KEY Pattern

Terminal Impulse — The Exhaustion Wedge

The most powerful reversal signal in NEoWave. A 5-wave impulse where ALL sub-waves are 3-wave structures (1:3, 2:3, 3:3, 4:3, 5:3) fitting inside a converging wedge. When complete: SHARP REVERSAL follows. This is "TERMINAL?" on the Bitcoin chart.

🧒 Think of a Tired Runner About to Collapse

Imagine a runner in a race who's nearly out of energy. Their steps get shorter and shorter (each wave gets smaller), they're barely moving forward anymore, but they're still going. That's a Terminal Impulse — the market is making one last push, but you can see it's running out of steam. Each sub-wave is a 3-wave corrective move (not a proper impulse), which means the trend is very weak. When the 5th tiny wave completes — the runner falls! A BIG REVERSAL is coming. This is why Trading Heights put "TERMINAL?" on the Bitcoin chart at the top!

Terminal Impulse Rules (NEoWave) — Most Strict

  • ALL 5 sub-waves must be 3-wave structures (corrective, not impulse). Hence labeled 1:3, 2:3, 3:3, 4:3, 5:3
  • Wave 3 must be shorter than Wave 1 (opposite of normal impulse!)
  • Wave 5 must be shorter than Wave 3
  • Wave 4 MUST overlap Wave 1's price territory (again, opposite of normal!)
  • All 5 waves must fit inside a converging channel / wedge
  • Wave 4 is typically larger in time/price than Wave 2
  • Upon completion → expect the sharpest and fastest counter-trend move of any pattern

Complex Correction Connector

X-Wave — The Pattern Connector

X-waves connect two separate corrective patterns in a "complex correction." They're the glue between patterns and tell you that what you're seeing is more complex than a simple correction. Identified by their shallow, 50–79% retracement.

🧒 Think of a Rest Stop Between Two Journeys

Imagine you're driving a long road trip. Between two cities you have a rest stop where you just stretch your legs a bit — you don't go very far off the highway. An X-wave is exactly that rest stop in the market. The price makes a small, shallow move (only 50-79% back) BETWEEN two corrective patterns. On the Bitcoin chart, after the Expanding Triangle/Diametric pattern, there's a small "g·x" connector before the Flat correction begins. That "x" is the X-wave!

X-Wave — Connecting two corrective patterns — Shallow retracement (50-79% of prior wave)

X-Wave Rules (NEoWave)

  • Always a 3-wave corrective structure (never 5-wave)
  • Retraces 50% to 79% of the prior corrective pattern's size
  • Cannot retrace more than 100% of the prior pattern
  • Typically much shorter in time than the patterns it connects
  • When you see an X-wave, expect another corrective pattern to follow (same length or larger)
  • Complex corrections: Double-three (W-X-Y), Triple-three (W-X-Y-X-Z)

🔑 How to Find X-Waves

After a complex-looking corrective pattern, if price retraces only 50-79% and then continues in the original direction, label that shallow move as "x". It's the market catching its breath between two larger corrective phases. On Bitcoin's chart, the "g·x" label on the arrow marks exactly this connector.

Mathematics of Waves

NEoWave Fibonacci Ratios — The Market's DNA

Glenn Neely discovered that markets use specific Fibonacci relationships more than others. These ratios appear consistently across all timeframes and all markets. Knowing them gives you exact price targets.

🧒 Think of Nature's Secret Code

Have you ever seen a sunflower? Its seeds spiral in the golden ratio — a special number (1.618...) that appears everywhere in nature: seashells, galaxies, even the human face! Markets also seem to follow this same hidden code. Wave corrections and extensions almost always end at specific ratio levels — like 38.2%, 61.8%, or 161.8% of the previous wave. NEoWave uses these ratios to predict EXACTLY where price will stop or target next. It's like the market has a hidden ruler!

NEoWave Fibonacci Rules

  • Wave 2 retraces 38.2% to 61.8% of Wave 1 in most cases
  • Wave 3 is typically 161.8% of Wave 1, or 61.8% of Waves 1+2 combined
  • Wave 4 retraces 38.2% of Wave 3 (most common)
  • Wave 5 often equals Wave 1, or 61.8% of Waves 1-3
  • B-wave of flat = 61.8% to 138.2% of Wave A
  • C-wave of flat = 127.2% or 161.8% beyond A's end
  • If a wave misses ALL Fibonacci levels → question your wave count!

🌻 Golden Ratio = 1.618

The Golden Ratio (φ = 1.618...) is the most important number in NEoWave. It appears as 61.8% retracements and 161.8% extensions. When a wave hits exactly 61.8%, prepare for a reversal. When it extends to 161.8%, that's your Wave 3 target.

Master Reference

NEoWave Core Rules — The Complete Cheat Sheet

These are the fundamental laws that Glenn Neely discovered. Every NEoWave analysis must follow ALL applicable rules. If even one rule is violated, it's a different pattern. Zero exceptions.

🧒 Think of Traffic Laws

Just like traffic laws — you can't run a red light "just a little bit" — NEoWave rules are absolute. If Wave 3 is shorter than Wave 1 AND Wave 5, it's NOT an impulse. It's like saying "I barely ran the red light" — it's still a violation! These rules remove all the guesswork from wave counting. When all rules are satisfied → high confidence trade!

The 5 Laws of NEoWave Pattern Formation

01
Law of Pattern IntegrityEvery wave must meet ALL structure requirements. A pattern with one violated rule is INVALID — not "mostly valid."
02
Law of Wave AlternationIn any 5-wave pattern, Wave 2 and Wave 4 must alternate in form — one is simple (zigzag), the other is complex (flat/triangle).
03
Law of Wave ProportionWave 3 can NEVER be the shortest of waves 1, 3, and 5 in an impulse. Wave 3 is usually the longest.
04
Law of Channel ContainmentEach wave pattern must fit within a parallel or converging price channel. If it breaks the channel, question the count.
05
Law of Fibonacci ConfluenceTrue wave endpoints must align with Fibonacci ratios of the prior wave. If NO Fibonacci level is hit, the wave count is suspect.
06
Law of Position LogicWhich patterns are allowed depends on WHERE in the larger structure you are. A terminal can only occur as the last wave of a pattern.
