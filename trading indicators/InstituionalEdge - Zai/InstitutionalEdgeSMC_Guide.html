Market Structure — BOS & CHoCH

Break of Structure & Change of Character Detection
▼

Market structure is the foundational edge that every prop trader starts with. It tells you the directional bias of the market — whether institutions are accumulating for a move up or distributing for a move down. Without structure, every other edge is blind.

Break of Structure (BOS) occurs when price breaks a swing point in the direction of the existing trend. For example, in a bullish trend, when price breaks above the previous swing high, that's a BOS — it confirms the trend is continuing. This is institutions adding to their positions and pushing the market in the direction they want it to go. A BOS tells you the trend is intact and you should be looking for entries in that direction only.

Change of Character (CHoCH) is the early warning that the trend is reversing. It happens when price breaks a swing point AGAINST the existing trend. In a bullish trend, when price breaks below the previous swing low, that's a bearish CHoCH — institutions have stopped buying and started selling. This is the single most important signal for catching trend reversals early, before the crowd realizes what's happening. The indicator marks these with distinct labels and colors.

How This Indicator Detects Structure:

  • Uses configurable pivot lookback (default: 5 bars) to identify swing highs and lows
  • Tracks the sequence of swing points to determine if structure is bullish (higher highs/higher lows) or bearish (lower highs/lower lows)
  • BOS signals are plotted when price breaks a swing in the direction of the current trend
  • CHoCH signals are plotted when price breaks a swing against the current trend — this updates the trend direction
  • The structure trend feeds directly into the confluence engine — bullish structure adds +1 to bull score, bearish adds +1 to bear score

Order Blocks — Institutional Accumulation Zones

Where Smart Money Entered the Market
▼

Order blocks are the footprints left by institutional orders. When a bank or fund wants to buy 100,000 shares, they can't just click "buy" like retail traders — their order would move the price against them. Instead, they accumulate slowly, often using the last opposing candle before a strong impulse move as their entry zone. That candle IS the order block.

A Bullish Order Block is the last bearish candle before a strong bullish impulse. This is where institutions were buying while the crowd was selling. When price returns to this zone, it often bounces because there's still unfilled institutional buy demand there. The indicator draws these as blue boxes on the chart.

A Bearish Order Block is the last bullish candle before a strong bearish impulse. This is where institutions were selling into retail buying. When price returns to this zone, it often rejects because there's still institutional sell supply there. These are drawn as red boxes.

Detection Logic:

  • Identifies strong impulse moves (body > 0.8x ATR) — these are institutional footprints
  • The candle before the impulse (opposite direction) is marked as the Order Block
  • OB zones extend using ATR multiplier (configurable, default: 2.0x) to account for institutional scale
  • Only the most recent OBs are kept (configurable max, default: 5) to avoid chart clutter
  • Price touching an OB zone adds +1 to the corresponding confluence score

Fair Value Gaps — Imbalance Entry Zones

Price Imbalances That Get Filled
▼

Fair Value Gaps (FVGs) are three-candle patterns where a gap exists between the high of the first candle and the low of the third candle (for bullish FVG) or between the low of the first candle and the high of the third candle (for bearish FVG). This gap represents a price imbalance — an area where price moved so fast that it left a "hole" in the market.

The key insight about FVGs is that the market tends to fill these gaps. Why? Because institutional algorithms are programmed to provide liquidity in imbalanced areas. When they see a gap, they step in to fill it, which creates a natural price magnet. This is why FVGs are such powerful entry zones — you're entering at a point where price is statistically likely to visit.

The prop firm edge with FVGs is not just in identifying them — it's in trading them in the direction of the institutional order flow. A bullish FVG that forms after a liquidity sweep, inside an order block, in the direction of the market structure? That's a high-probability setup. The indicator detects these and feeds them into the confluence engine.

Detection Logic:

  • Bullish FVG: Current bar's low > two-bars-ago's high (gap between bar[2] high and current bar low)
  • Bearish FVG: Current bar's high < two-bars-ago's low (gap between bar[2] low and current bar high)
  • Only valid when the impulse candle closes in the direction of the gap (bullish candle for bull FVG)
  • FVG boxes are drawn on chart and persist until price fills them or max count is reached
  • Price inside an unfilled FVG adds +1 to the corresponding confluence score

Liquidity Sweeps — The Stop Hunt Signal

Institutions Grabbing Your Stops Before The Real Move
▼

This is arguably the most important edge in the entire system. A liquidity sweep is when price briefly moves beyond an obvious swing high or swing low — just enough to trigger stop losses — and then immediately reverses. This is not random. This is institutions engineering a move to grab liquidity (your stops) so they can fill their massive orders at better prices.

Think about it: when you place your stop loss below a swing low, you're not the only one. Thousands of retail traders have their stops at the exact same level. That's a massive pool of sell orders just sitting there. Institutions know this and they exploit it. They push price below the swing low, all those sell stops are triggered (which are actually buy orders for the institution), and then they reverse the market upward. Your stop loss was their fuel.

The indicator detects this by checking if price has wicked beyond a recent swing extreme (using a configurable lookback) and then closed back inside. The wick must be at least 0.3x ATR beyond the level to filter out noise. When a bullish liquidity sweep is detected (price sweeps below a low then closes above it), it's one of the strongest signals that institutions have just grabbed sell-side liquidity and are about to push price higher. This alone can provide a significant edge.

Detection Logic:

  • Tracks the highest high and lowest low over a configurable lookback (default: 20 bars)
  • Bullish sweep: low wicks below the tracked low pool, then closes above it
  • Bearish sweep: high wicks above the tracked high pool, then closes below it
  • Minimum sweep depth: 0.3x ATR beyond the level (filters out minor wicks)
  • Liquidity sweep detection adds +1 to the corresponding confluence score
  • Marked on chart with diamond labels at the sweep point

Volume Anomaly Detection — Institutional Footprints

Volume Spikes That Reveal Smart Money Activity
▼

Volume is the one indicator that institutions cannot hide. They can manipulate price with wicks and fakeouts, but when they execute large orders, volume spikes. These spikes are the undeniable footprints of institutional activity. The key is not just spotting any volume spike, but understanding what the volume spike means in context.

The indicator calculates a volume moving average (default: 20-period) and flags bars where volume exceeds a configurable threshold (default: 2x the MA). But it doesn't stop there — it also checks the direction of the bar. A volume spike on a bullish candle (close > open) with price closing higher than the previous close indicates institutional buying pressure. A volume spike on a bearish candle indicates institutional selling pressure. This directional context is crucial.

When a volume anomaly coincides with other edges — say, a bullish volume spike at an order block after a liquidity sweep — the probability of a successful trade increases dramatically. This is why volume anomaly is a key component of the confluence engine. It confirms that the move has institutional backing, not just retail noise.

Detection Logic:

  • Calculates 20-period simple moving average of volume
  • Flags bars where volume > threshold × MA (default threshold: 2.0x)
  • Bullish anomaly: volume spike + bullish candle + higher close than previous
  • Bearish anomaly: volume spike + bearish candle + lower close than previous
  • Plots colored dots below/above bars to mark anomalies
  • Volume anomaly detection adds +1 to the corresponding confluence score

VWAP + Deviation Bands — Fair Value & Mean Reversion

The Institutional Benchmark for Fair Price
▼

VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) is the single most important reference point for institutional traders. It represents the average price paid for an asset, weighted by volume. When price is above VWAP, buyers are in control. When below, sellers dominate. Institutions use VWAP to benchmark their execution — if they're buying below VWAP, they're getting a good deal relative to the market average.

The standard deviation bands around VWAP are where the real edge lives. When price reaches the -2σ band, it's at a statistical extreme — price is 2 standard deviations below the volume-weighted mean. In a normally distributed market, price should be within 2σ about 95% of the time. When it touches the -2σ band and starts reversing, that's a high-probability mean reversion setup. Institutions know this and often accumulate at these extremes.

The indicator uses VWAP as a directional filter: price below VWAP adds +1 to bullish confluence (because the edge is buying below fair value), and price above VWAP adds +1 to bearish confluence. Price at the ±2σ bands provides the strongest mean reversion signals. This is the statistical edge that quant firms build their entire business around.

How the VWAP Edge Works:

  • VWAP is calculated using TradingView's built-in ta.vwap function on HLC3
  • Two sets of deviation bands: Band 1 (default: ±1σ) and Band 2 (default: ±2σ)
  • Price below VWAP adds +1 to bullish confluence (buying below fair value)
  • Price above VWAP adds +1 to bearish confluence (selling above fair value)
  • Price touching ±2σ bands provides the strongest mean reversion edge
  • Bands are drawn with distinct styling to differentiate from price action

How to Install & Configure

Follow these steps to get the Institutional Edge SMC indicator running on your TradingView chart.

1

Copy the Pine Script Code

Open the file InstitutionalEdgeSMC.pine that was provided alongside this guide. Select all the code and copy it to your clipboard. Make sure you copy the entire file from the first line (// This Pine Script™ v5...) to the last line. Missing even a single character will cause compilation errors.

2

Open TradingView Pine Editor

Go to TradingView.com, open any chart, and click on the "Pine Editor" tab at the bottom of the screen. If you don't see it, click the "Editor" button in the top toolbar. The Pine Editor is where you write and manage your custom indicators.

3

Paste and Save

Delete any existing code in the Pine Editor, paste the copied code, and click "Save" (or press Ctrl+S). The script should compile without errors. If you see any errors, double-check that you copied the entire file. The indicator name should appear as "Institutional Edge SMC [Prop Firm Grade]" in the editor title bar.

4

Add to Chart

Click "Add to Chart" in the Pine Editor. The indicator will appear on your chart with all six edge components active. You'll see the VWAP line, order block boxes, FVG zones, structure labels, volume anomaly dots, liquidity sweep markers, and the confluence table in the top-right corner.

5

Configure Settings

Click the gear icon on the indicator to access settings. The most important settings are: Min Confluence Score (default: 3 — increase to 4 for fewer but higher-quality signals), Volume Spike Threshold (default: 2.0x — increase in volatile markets), and SL/TP ATR Multipliers (defaults: 1.5x SL, 3.0x TP for a 1:2 risk/reward ratio).

6

Recommended Timeframes & Assets

This indicator works best on 15-minute, 1-hour, and 4-hour timeframes. Lower timeframes (1m, 5m) have too much noise and false signals. Higher timeframes (daily+) work but signals are rare. Best assets: Forex majors (EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY), indices (NQ, ES), gold (XAUUSD), and Bitcoin. These have the deepest institutional liquidity, which is what the indicator is designed to detect.

Is this really a "hidden" strategy? It seems like common SMC concepts. +

What win rate can I expect? +

Honestly? It depends entirely on the market, the timeframe, the asset, and your execution. The ICT Silver Bullet model (a subset of what this indicator does) has been backtested at approximately 77% win rate with 1:2 R:R. However, past performance does not guarantee future results. Should I increase the minimum confluence score for better quality? +

Yes, and here's the trade-off: A minimum score of 3/6 gives you more signals but with lower individual quality. A minimum of 4/6 gives you fewer signals but each one has a higher probability of success. A minimum of 5/6 is extremely selective — you might get only 1-2 signals per week on the 1-hour timeframe, but they're very high probability. For beginners, start with 3/6 and manually filter the signals using the confluence table. As you gain experience, increase to 4/6. For prop firm challenges, 4/6 or 5/6 is recommended to maintain consistency.

Can I use this for scalping on the 1-minute chart? +

Technically yes, but it's not recommended. The 1-minute timeframe has too much market noise, and the spread/commission costs eat into your profits disproportionately. The order block and FVG detection becomes less reliable on very low timeframes because institutional footprints are harder to distinguish from noise. The sweet spot is the 15-minute to 4-hour range. If you want to scalp, use the 15-minute as your signal timeframe and the 1-minute as your entry timeframe (multi-timeframe analysis). This gives you the institutional edge on the higher timeframe while getting precise entries on the lower one.

Does this repaint? Can I trust the signals? +

No, this indicator does not repaint. All signals are generated on bar close, which means once a signal appears, it stays. The BOS/CHoCH labels use confirmed pivot points (which require lookback bars), so they appear a few bars after the actual event — this is standard and not repainting. The order blocks and FVGs are drawn when the pattern completes and persist until price mitigates them or the max count is reached. The confluence table updates in real-time but only reflects the current bar's state. Always verify signals on bar close, not during bar formation, for the most reliable entries.

What's the most important thing to remember? +

Risk management is more important than any indicator. Even the best edge in the world will lose money if you risk too much per trade, revenge trade after losses, or abandon your rules during drawdowns. This indicator gives you an edge in identifying high-probability setups — but it cannot discipline you. That has to come from within. Follow the trading rules in the section above. Respect the daily and weekly loss limits. Never risk more than 1-2% per trade. The traders who make it in this business are not the ones with the best indicators — they're the ones with the best discipline. Be one of them.
