SETUP 01
Order Blocks
CORE SETUP Swing Intraday ELITE
74%
WIN RATE
What Is It?

An Order Block (OB) is the last opposing candle before a significant impulsive move (displacement). It represents the price level where institutional orders were placed — and where algorithms will return to fill remaining orders. It's the institution's "fingerprint" on the chart.


🟢 Bullish OB = Last bearish candle before a strong upward move.
🔴 Bearish OB = Last bullish candle before a strong downward move.

Entry Checklist (5 Rules)
1

Identify the higher timeframe bias (Daily/4H). Only trade OBs in the direction of the premium/discount array.

2

Wait for liquidity sweep before expecting OB to react. Institutions need fuel (stops) to reverse.

3

Drop to lower timeframe (5m/15m) to see price return to the OB with precision entry.

4

Enter when price returns to the 50% midline or lower quarter of the OB. Aggressive = OB touch; Conservative = confirmation candle.

5

SL below the full OB body (not wick). TP at previous high/low, opposing FVG, or 1:3R minimum.

💡 Pro Insight (Hidden from Retail)

Institutions do NOT place orders at round numbers. They place them at the open of the last opposing candle before displacement. The "50% of OB" (the midline) is the highest probability entry because it's where algorithms refill remaining inventory.

HIGH FREQ Scalp Intraday ELITE

What Is It?

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) — also called imbalance or inefficiency — forms when price moves so aggressively in 3 candles that the wicks don't overlap: high[2] < low[0] (bullish) or low[2] > high[0] (bearish). This creates a price void that algorithms are PROGRAMMED to return to and fill before continuing.

Entry Checklist
1

Spot a 3-candle gap after a displacement move. The gap = the space between candle 1 high and candle 3 low (bullish) or vice versa.

2

Wait for price to retrace INTO the FVG. Aggressive entry = top of FVG; Safe entry = 50% of FVG (Equilibrium point).

3

Best entries: FVG inside a Kill Zone + FVG aligned with higher-timeframe OB = institutional confluence stack.

4

SL below/above the full FVG. TP: next draw on liquidity (opposite FVG, old high/low, or institutional level).

What Is It?

A Breaker Block is a failed Order Block that flips polarity. When price sweeps through an OB that couldn't hold, that zone transforms: a bullish OB that failed becomes a bearish Breaker, and a bearish OB that failed becomes a bullish Breaker. Institutions use the liquidity from those failed OB stops to load the new direction.

Entry Checklist
1

Identify an OB that was violated — price closed THROUGH the OB rather than respecting it.

2

Mark the zone of the original OB. This is now the Breaker block zone.

3

Wait for price to return to the Breaker zone from the other side. The failed support is now resistance, and vice versa.

4

Enter on a lower-TF displacement away from the Breaker. SL beyond the original OB extreme.

What Is It?

Liquidity Sweeps (Stop Hunts) are the single most important ICT concept. All traders place stop losses above swing highs and below swing lows. Institutions NEED this liquidity (volume) to fill their large positions. So they engineer a false breakout to collect stops, then reverse — leaving retail traders wondering why "the breakout failed."


🟡 BSL (Buy-Side Liq) = Stop clusters above recent swing highs.
🟡 SSL (Sell-Side Liq) = Stop clusters below recent swing lows.

Entry Checklist
1

Mark all significant swing highs and lows — these are liquidity pools. Equal highs/lows (EQH/EQL) are especially strong targets.

2

Wait for price to spike above/below the level and CLOSE back below/above it within the same or next candle.

3

Confirm: the sweep should happen inside a Kill Zone (London/NY open) for maximum probability.

4

Enter after close back inside the level with SL above the sweep wick. Target: opposite liquidity pool.

📈 After SSL Sweep (Long)

Stop LossBelow sweep wick low
TargetBSL above / FVG
R:R1:3 – 1:6

📉 After BSL Sweep (Short)

TriggerHigh swept, close below
EntryRe-close below BSL level
Stop LossAbove sweep wick high
TargetSSL below / FVG
R:R1:3 – 1:6
💡 Pro Insight (Hidden from Retail)

The #1 reason retail traders lose is placing stops at obvious levels. Every trader using basic technical analysis places stops at the same places — swing highs/lows, round numbers, moving averages. Institutions know exactly where these are because they can SEE the order book. They engineer a move to these levels specifically to take your stop and use that liquidity to enter their real position. Trade WITH the sweep, not against it.

SETUP 05
Market Structure (BOS / CHoCH)
Swing Trend Filter STRONG
72%
WIN RATE
What Is It?

BOS (Break of Structure) = Price breaks above the most recent swing high (bullish) or below swing low (bearish) — confirming trend continuation. CHoCH (Change of Character) = The first opposite-direction BOS after a trend — indicating a potential reversal. This is the FOUNDATION of all ICT analysis — you must know the structure before taking any setup.

Structure Rules
1

Bullish Structure: Higher Highs (HH) + Higher Lows (HL). Only take LONGS. Short setups are countertrend.

2

Bearish Structure: Lower Lows (LL) + Lower Highs (LH). Only take SHORTS. Long setups are countertrend.

3

CHoCH is the EARLIEST signal of reversal. After CHoCH, wait for the first retracement OB/FVG to confirm the new direction.

4

EQH/EQL (Equal Highs/Lows) = Liquidity magnets. When price forms double tops/bottoms, smart money WILL sweep them before reversing.

What Is It?

The Asian Range (18:00–00:00 NY / 23:00–05:00 GMT) is the daily accumulation phase. During Asia, price consolidates in a tight range. London/NY sessions EXPAND out of this range. The Asian High and Low become key liquidity levels — London almost always sweeps one side before the real move begins.

Entry Checklist
1

Mark the Asian session High and Low from the previous session (or current if still in Asia).

2

Wait for London open. Watch for a sweep of the Asian High or Low — this is the Judas Swing (manipulation phase).

3

After the sweep, look for a CHoCH on the lower timeframe (5m/15m) and an FVG or OB forming in the opposite direction.

4

Trade the REVERSAL: if Asian High swept → SHORT. If Asian Low swept → LONG. TP = opposite range extreme + 50–100% extension.

💡 Pro Insight

The Asian Range gives you the daily game plan every morning before London opens. 70%+ of trading days follow this pattern: Asia consolidates → London sweeps one extreme → NY moves in the opposite direction. The key is WHICH side gets swept at London open. Once you see the sweep, the direction of the day is almost certain.

The 3 Phases Explained
①

Accumulation (Asia): Market consolidates in a tight range. Institutions are quietly building positions. No clear direction. This creates the "coiled spring."

②

Manipulation (London): The Judas Swing. Price moves OPPOSITE to the final direction to sweep stops and grab liquidity. This is the "fake move" that traps retail traders.

③

Distribution (New York): The real institutional move. Price rockets in the TRUE direction, completely opposite to the Judas Swing. Retail traders who got stopped are watching in disbelief.

How to Trade PO3
A

During Asia: Mark the range. No trading. Just observe and plan.

B

At London open: Watch for the Judas Swing. If price sweeps the Asian HIGH → expect the day to go LOWER (bearish). If sweeps Asian LOW → expect HIGHER (bullish).

C

During NY open: Enter in the TRUE direction with OB or FVG entry. This is when the distribution phase creates the cleanest intraday trend.
