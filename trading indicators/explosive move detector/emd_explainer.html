// THREE PILLARS

The engine under the hood.

Each pillar measures a different dimension of market behavior. All three must align for a confirmed signal.

📐

VOLATILITY

Measures how violently price is moving relative to its own history. Uses True Range (the widest of bar range, high-to-prev-close, and low-to-prev-close) normalized against a rolling ATR baseline.

vol_ratio = TR ÷ SMA(TR, lookback)
spike ✓ if vol_ratio ≥ threshold

A ratio of 2.0 means the bar's range is twice the recent average. This catches breakouts, liquidity sweeps, and news-driven candles.

🚀

MOMENTUM

Measures the speed of directional price movement, not just range. Uses Rate of Change: close minus close[n] bars ago, compared to its own rolling mean magnitude.

mom_ratio = |ROC| ÷ SMA(|ROC|, lookback)
spike ✓ if mom_ratio ≥ threshold

Direction is captured separately — positive ROC = bullish burst, negative = bearish. This prevents large-range doji candles (high vol, zero momentum) from triggering false positives.

📊

VOLUME (RVOL)

The confirmation gate. A price move without volume is noise; a price move with elevated volume is conviction. Relative Volume (RVOL) compares current 1-min volume to its rolling average.

rvol = volume ÷ SMA(volume, lookback)
confirmed ✓ if rvol ≥ threshold

Without volume confirmation, signals show as yellow (unconfirmed). Volume confirmation is the final gate that separates real moves from thin-market noise.

// INPUT SETTINGS

What every knob does.

Six inputs control the entire detector. Here's when and why to adjust each one.

vol_lookback
DEFAULT: 100 bars
How many 1-minute bars define "normal" volatility. Larger = slower baseline adaptation, more resistant to regime changes. Smaller = faster adaptation, catches shifting volatility regimes (e.g., post-earnings).

Try: 50 for fast stocks, 200 for indices or slow instruments.
vol_threshold
DEFAULT: 2.0×
Minimum volatility ratio to flag a spike. 2.0 = bar's true range is twice the average — already quite unusual. Lower to 1.5 for more signals; raise to 3.0 for only extreme events (e.g., circuit breakers, flash crashes).

Never: set below 1.2 — too many false positives.
mom_lookback
DEFAULT: 14 bars
The lookback period for Rate of Change: measures close vs. close[n]. 14 bars = 14 minutes of directional movement. Shorter values (5–8) detect very short-term momentum bursts; longer (20–30) detect sustained trending momentum.
mom_threshold
DEFAULT: 2.0×
Minimum momentum ratio to flag abnormal speed. Works the same way as vol_threshold but for directional movement. Keep this equal to or slightly above vol_threshold to avoid momentum dominating the composite signal.
rvol_lookback
DEFAULT: 50 bars
Baseline window for average 1-minute volume. 50 bars ≈ last ~50 minutes of volume. Shorter windows react faster to intraday volume shifts; longer windows compare against a more stable "session average." Consider 100+ for equities with clear session volume patterns.
rvol_threshold
DEFAULT: 1.5×
Volume must exceed this multiple of the rolling average to confirm a signal. 1.5 is intentionally lower than the vol/mom thresholds — volume surges slightly before or alongside price in genuine explosive moves. Raise to 2.0 for very liquid instruments where 1.5× is routine.
// USAGE GUIDE

How to actually trade this.

The indicator is a context detector, not an entry system. Here's how to integrate it properly.

  • 01. Add it as a separate pane below your main chart. Never overlay it — the histogram values are ratios (dimensionless), not price. The orange dashed line is your threshold; anything above it is elevated.
  • 02. Green confirmed = momentum entry window. When a bullish explosive bar fires, the next 1–3 minutes often show continuation. Use it to time entries during breakouts, not as a standalone signal. Look left for a support level or a base — explosive moves from structure are more reliable.
  • 03. Yellow = stand by, not act. An unconfirmed spike (yellow bar) means price is moving but institutions aren't behind it yet. Watch the cyan RVOL line. If it crosses its dashed threshold on the next bar while volatility is still elevated — that's your confirmation sequence in real time.
  • 04. Clusters of confirmed signals = regime change, not entry. If you see 5–8 consecutive green/red bars, the instrument has shifted into a high-volatility regime. Widen your stops. ATR-based sizing becomes critical here. Avoid chasing — the move is likely already extended.
  • 05. First N bars of the session will show false readings. The rolling baselines need vol_lookback and rvol_lookback bars to populate. On a fresh session or symbol load, wait 50–100 bars before trusting the signals. The indicator inherits Pine Script's built-in na() guard for these early bars.
  • 06. Low-liquidity instruments need higher thresholds. Penny stocks, small-cap futures, or crypto altcoins naturally have erratic volume. Raise vol_threshold to 3.0 and rvol_threshold to 2.5 to cut through the noise. The defaults are calibrated for liquid instruments (large-cap equities, major FX pairs, index futures).
  • 07. Alerts are your real-time trigger. Set TradingView alerts on "Bullish Explosive Move" and "Bearish Explosive Move" with webhook or push notification. The alert message includes the ticker, close price, and ratio values so you have full context without watching the chart.

// NO-REPAINT GUARANTEE

The indicator uses request.security(..., lookahead=barmerge.lookahead_off) which ensures Pine Script never uses future-bar data to compute current-bar values. What you see on a historical bar is exactly what you would have seen in real time. This is the most critical anti-repaint safeguard in Pine Script.
