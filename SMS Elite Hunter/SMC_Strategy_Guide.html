The Hidden Truth About Markets
The Fundamental Reality

Retail traders lose because they trade with the crowd. When everyone buys, who sells? When everyone places a stop at the same level — who profits when price hits it? Institutions.

Markets are not random. They are liquidity delivery systems. Banks and hedge funds need millions of contracts filled. To fill a 10,000-lot buy order, someone must sell — and they manufacture that selling pressure by engineering a fake break below obvious support, triggering stop-losses that become their buy orders.

Once filled, they reveal their true direction via a violent displacement candle. The Fair Value Gap (imbalance) left in that candle is where they add more. That's your entry.

What Retail Traders See

Price breaks support → go short

Support holds → go long

Trend line breaks → exit

Result: Getting stopped out repeatedly at "obvious" levels

What Institutions Actually Do

Accumulate quietly below resistance

Sweep obvious stop levels to fill orders

Displacement confirms direction

Result: Entering at the exact point retail exits

The 3-Phase Market Model
PHASE 1
🏗️
ACCUMULATION
Institutions quietly build positions while keeping price in a range. Retail sees "consolidation."
PHASE 2
🏹
MANIPULATION
Stop hunt. Price sweeps the obvious level. Retail gets stopped out. Institutions get filled.
PHASE 3
🚀
DISTRIBUTION
Violent displacement. Price moves toward target liquidity. Smart money profits.
💡
Your job as a retail trader is NOT to predict direction. It is to wait for the manipulation phase to complete, confirm the displacement, and enter in Phase 3 — riding the institutional wave to the next liquidity target.

The 5 Components Explained
① Market Structure — BOS & CHoCH

Break of Structure (BOS) — Trend continuation. When price is in an uptrend and closes above the previous swing high, structure is intact. Trade WITH the bias.

Change of Character (CHoCH) — Trend reversal warning. When price breaks the opposing swing extreme, the bias has flipped. Only take trades in the new direction after CHoCH confirmation.

ℹ️
Always establish HTF (1H/4H/Daily) bias FIRST before entering on 15m. A bullish CHoCH on the 15m is noise if the 4H is screaming bearish BOS.
② Fair Value Gaps (FVG)

An FVG is created when a single candle moves so aggressively that it leaves an unfilled price imbalance — a gap between candle 1's high and candle 3's low (bullish) or candle 1's low and candle 3's high (bearish).

FVG LOGIC
// ─── Bullish FVG ─────────────────────────────── Candle[2].high < Candle[0].low // Gap exists above // Price will return to fill this gap from ABOVE // = institutional limit buy orders sitting here // ─── Bearish FVG ─────────────────────────────── Candle[2].low > Candle[0].high // Gap exists below // Price will return to fill this gap from BELOW

The best FVGs are: (1) created inside a Kill Zone session, (2) on the same TF as your entry, (3) unfilled / fresh, (4) in the discount/premium zone aligned with bias.

③ Order Blocks (OB)

An order block is the last opposing candle before a displacement move. It represents the zone where institutions placed their orders. When price returns to it, those same orders fire again.

TYPEDEFINITIONENTRY ZONE
Bullish OB Last bearish (red) candle before a strong bullish displacement move High of the bearish candle → Low of the bearish candle
Bearish OB Last bullish (green) candle before a strong bearish displacement move Low of the bullish candle → High of the bullish candle
⚠️
Order blocks are CONSUMED on the first touch. Never enter on a second or third return to the same OB — the orders have been filled and the OB is dead. The indicator automatically greys them out when mitigated.
④ Liquidity Pools — BSL & SSL

BSL (BuySide Liquidity) — Equal or near-equal highs. Every retail trader who sold at this level has their stop above it. Institutions will sweep it to fill their sell orders. When you see BSL, expect a spike above → rejection.

SSL (SellSide Liquidity) — Equal or near-equal lows. Every retail buyer has their stop below it. Institutions sweep it to fill their buy orders. When you see SSL, expect a spike below → reversal.

The liq_tol parameter (default 0.08%) controls how close two levels must be to count as "equal." Tighten it for cleaner signals on higher timeframes.

⑤ Entry Signals — The Synthesis

The signal fires only when ALL conditions align: liquidity is swept, displacement is confirmed, and the candle closes in the right direction with enough body to prove conviction. This is the stop-hunt fingerprint of institutional order flow.

The Exact Trade Setup — Step by Step
The 6-Step Trade Framework
  1. Establish HTF Bias
    Open the 4H or Daily chart. Identify the last CHoCH. Only take trades in that direction on the 15m. A bullish 4H bias means you only hunt LONG setups on the entry timeframe — no exceptions.
  2. Identify the Nearest Liquidity Pool
    Look for SSL (equal lows below price) for long trades, or BSL (equal highs above price) for shorts. This is what institutions are targeting. You are predicting their next move.
  3. Wait for the Sweep Candle
    The candle must wick THROUGH the liquidity level but CLOSE back on the correct side. A candle that merely touches the level and closes through it is NOT a sweep — it's a breakout. The close must reclaim the level.
  4. Confirm Displacement
    The candle body must be ≥ 0.4× ATR(14). Small doji bodies after a sweep are weak and unreliable. You want a candle that SCREAMS conviction — big body, small wicks, in the right direction.
  5. Mark the FVG / Order Block
    The displacement candle leaves behind a Fair Value Gap. This is your entry zone. Set a limit order at the 50% level of the FVG. If no FVG, the Order Block (last opposing candle) is your entry zone. Enter at the midpoint of the OB.
  6. Set SL Beyond the Wick + Target BSL/SSL
    Stop loss goes BELOW the sweep candle wick (for longs) + 0.25× ATR buffer. Target is the opposite liquidity pool (BSL for longs). Minimum 2R required. If the math doesn't work, skip the trade.

London Open (2–5 AM EST)

The most powerful session. European banks open and immediately hunt the liquidity built during the Asian session. Expect sweeps of Asian session highs or lows within the first 30 minutes of this window.

Best pairs: EURUSD, GBPUSD, EURGBP

Common pattern: Asian range forms → London sweeps the high or low → displacement → entry

New York Open (7–10 AM EST)

NY banks often reverse London's move OR continue with a second powerful impulse. The overlap of London and NY (7–9 AM) is the highest-volume window of the day — the cleanest FVGs and most reliable sweeps occur here.

Best instruments: NAS100, SPX500, BTCUSD, DXY pairs

Common pattern: Price consolidates AM → NY sweep of London's high/low → strong trend begins

⚠️
News Events Kill the Strategy: Never trade within 30 minutes of high-impact news (CPI, NFP, FOMC). The liquidity sweeps during news are driven by algorithmic stop-running, not the clean institutional footprint this strategy requires. Mark your economic calendar and sit out.

Risk Management — The Actual Rules
The Golden Rules (Non-Negotiable)
RULEVALUEREASONING
Max risk per trade 1% of account At 60% win rate with 2R average: 100 trades = +80R. With 2% risk, a 5-trade losing streak = -10%. Unrecoverable mentally.
Minimum R:R to enter 2.0R At 55% win rate with 2R: expectancy = 0.55×2 - 0.45×1 = +0.65R per trade. Profitable even if you're wrong 45% of the time.
Max open trades 2 simultaneously Correlated markets (EURUSD + GBPUSD) move together. 2 trades effectively = 2% risk on one directional move.
Daily loss limit -3% and done 3 losing trades at 1% = done for the day. Revenge trading after 3 losses destroys accounts. No exceptions.
Breakeven move After +1R Once trade reaches 1R profit, move SL to entry. Now risk-free. Let it run to full target without stress.
Partial take profit 50% at +1.5R Lock in half at 1.5R, trail remaining position to 3R+. Best of both: certainty + upside capture.

The 8 Mistakes That Kill Accounts
✗
Trading against the HTF biasGoing long on 15m when the 4H is in a bearish BOS. The HTF trend will grind your setup into dust. Never fight the higher timeframe. Structure flows top-down: Daily → 4H → 1H → 15m → 5m.
✗
Entering before the candle closesThe sweep is only confirmed when the candle CLOSES back above/below the swept level. Entering on the wick mid-candle puts you in during the manipulation phase, not after it. Wait. The. Close.
✗
Moving the stop loss further awayYour stop is beyond the wick for a reason: if price returns to the sweep low/high, the setup is invalid. Moving SL to "give it more room" is not managing risk — it's hoping. Take the loss.
✗
Trading mitigated FVGs / OBsAn FVG that's been touched once has delivered its institutional orders. The second visit has no institutional support behind it. Grey zones on the indicator = dead zones. Don't enter.
✗
Ignoring session timingThe exact same setup in the dead zone (12–5 PM EST) will fail 65% of the time. Outside Kill Zones, there's no institutional participation to follow. The sweep happens but there's no fuel for continuation.
✗
Taking sub-2R tradesA 1.5R target with 60% win rate has expectancy of 0.50R/trade. A 2R target with 60% win rate has 0.80R/trade. Same effort, 60% more profit. Never compromise on minimum R:R.
✗
Risking more after a win streak"I've won 5 in a row so the next one is more likely to win." Wrong. Each trade is independent. Win streaks create overconfidence. This is where accounts get blown — not on losing streaks, but after winning ones.
✗
Forcing setups that don't existThe market does not owe you a trade. Some days the setups aren't clean. Some weeks the liquidity pools are ambiguous. Zero trades with zero losses is always better than forced trades with real losses. Patience is a trading edge.

Installation & Configuration Guide
Adding to TradingView
  1. Open Pine Script Editor
    In TradingView, click the "Pine Editor" tab at the bottom of the chart. This opens the code editor.
  2. Paste the Script
    Delete any existing code in the editor. Copy the full contents of SMC_Elite_Liquidity_Hunter.pine and paste it in.
  3. Save & Add to Chart
    Click "Save" (Ctrl+S), then click "Add to chart" (the play button). The indicator will appear on your active chart.
  4. Configure Settings
    Click the gear icon (⚙️) next to the indicator name. Set your preferred Pivot Length, FVG colors, and minimum R:R ratio based on the recommendations below.
  5. Set Up Alerts
    Right-click on the chart → "Add Alert". Select "SMC LONG — Liquidity Sweep" or "SMC SHORT — Liquidity Sweep" as the condition. Set to "Once Per Bar Close" for confirmed signals only.
