Understanding TPO

Institutional Market Profile & Time Price Opportunity

What is TPO?

Time Price Opportunity (TPO), commonly known as Market Profile, is an institutional charting technique developed by J. Peter Steidlmayer at the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT). Unlike traditional volume profiles that measure how many contracts traded at a price, TPO measures how much time the market spent at a price.

Our dynamic implementation divides the visible chart session into chronological segments. Prices that were heavily transacted accumulate horizontal graphical blocks. The thicker the block profile, the higher the market "acceptance" of that price.

Price Acceptance vs Time

Core Concepts

  • Value Area: The range of prices where approximately 70% of the session's time was spent. This represents "fair value".
  • Point of Control (POC): Red Dashed Line The single price level where the most blocks (time periods) occurred. This is the ultimate magnet for price.
  • Gradient Heatmap: Our TPO overlay colors blocks linearly (Blue → Green → Yellow → Red → Purple). The color tells you exactly when the volume came in during the session.
  • Single Prints: Areas on the profile where only a single horizontal block exists. These represent fast, emotional moves (imbalances) and often act as strong future support/resistance.

Institutional Trading Strategies

Retail traders focus on indicators; institutional traders focus on market structure and auction mechanics. TPO reveals the auction.

1. Value Area Rejection

If price opens outside the previous day's Value Area but quickly reverses and accepts back inside it, there is an 80% statistical probability it will traverse fully to the other side of the Value Area (The 80% Rule).

2. POC Magnetism

The POC acts as gravity. In ranging, non-trending markets, fade moves away from the prominent POC. Use the POC as your primary take-profit target for mean-reversion trades.

3. Single Print Fills

Thin areas in the profile (Single Prints) are vacuums. When price retraces into a Single Print zone, it will typically move aggressively through it due to a lack of historical liquidity in that zone.
